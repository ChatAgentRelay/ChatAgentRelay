Chat Agent Relay vs Building It Yourself

Compare · relay path

A custom integration can be fine when there is one chat surface, one agent, and a narrow operational scope. The difficulty usually appears later: a second channel, policy requirements, delivery failures, routing changes, or audit questions. At that point, teams are no longer just shipping an agent. They are rebuilding a relay path.

What is being compared

The comparison is not between "using AI" and "not using AI." It is between:

  • rebuilding the chat-to-agent message path inside each integration, or
  • using a standard relay layer centered on canonical events, governance, routing, A2A invocation, delivery reliability, replay, and audit.

The recurring costs of DIY

A working prototype often hides the long-term cost of the relay path. The recurring cost tends to come from the same places:

  • Provider-specific transport logic — signatures, callbacks, message shapes, rate limits, and delivery quirks repeat across platforms.
  • Lack of a stable internal contract — raw provider payloads and agent-specific response shapes leak into business logic.
  • Audit added late — correlation, causation, durable history, and blocked outcomes have to be retrofitted after incidents or compliance requests.
  • Error handling fragments — delivery failures, invocation failures, and policy blocks end up represented differently in different integrations.
  • Routing and governance drift — each integration grows its own local rules, which are hard to reason about consistently.

When DIY makes sense

CAR is not the only valid choice. Building in-house can be reasonable when:

  • you have one tightly scoped chat surface and one tightly scoped agent path,
  • you do not need a stable replayable canonical event model,
  • you are comfortable owning governance, routing, delivery, and audit behavior directly in application code, and
  • you accept that future expansion will likely require another round of integration work.

Why teams choose CAR

Teams adopt CAR when they want the relay path itself to be a first-class, governed, replayable system instead of an accumulation of one-off integrations.

  • Canonical events keep the internal contract stable.
  • Governance and routing stay on the message path instead of being reimplemented per channel.
  • A2A at the agent boundary keeps CAR focused on a standard agent-side protocol boundary rather than broad runtime abstraction.
  • Append-only replay and audit preserve what happened and why.
